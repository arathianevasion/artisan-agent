Retro after a run: what the wrapper adds, and one retro or two?

Sample run: plan "Timesheet export", 5 tickets in 3 waves, 2 gates, est. $14.00, actual $16.90 (+21%). The same fictional app as the review-packet prototype.

Upstream /retro reads one session's log and proposes navigation pointers, checks, coding standards, tool economy and information access. The wrapper adds: it reads the whole run (state file, gate actions, redirects, bypasses, overruns, findings); sorts each lesson into one of the eight homes; marks each "this project" or "generalises"; records its origin; checks the health of existing guardrails; asks about the Artisan's experience; and delivers the result (one PR, individual commits, pipeline tickets).

All three variants hold the same lessons. They differ in how many retros there are, when they happen, and whether you act on a packet or answer questions in a chat.
